# Week 16 — Challenges

The exercises build the machinery. **The challenge is the midterm itself, rehearsed.** You present your perception architecture brief to a panel and answer their questions against the rubric — the live architecture review that gates Phase 2.

## Index

1. **[Challenge 1 — Defend the perception stack to a panel](challenge-01-defend-the-perception-stack.md)** — present your block diagram, interface contract, latency budget, and failure-mode table to a panel (a peer, an instructor, or a self-administered checklist), and answer the standard architecture-review questions against the rubric. The dry run for the real midterm. (~2 hours)

This challenge is *not* optional in spirit, even though challenges are formally optional for passing the week — because the challenge **is** the format of the graded midterm. The skill it builds — presenting a perception stack you composed and defending its numbers to someone who didn't build it — is exactly what the Phase 2 hard gate measures, and exactly what you face again at Weeks 32, 40, and 48. A learner who rehearses the defense here walks into the real review calm, with a brief and the numbers ready; a learner who skips it discovers the panel's questions for the first time on stage, under grade pressure, with a half-working graph. Do the rehearsal. It is the cheapest insurance against the most consequential gate in Phase 2.
